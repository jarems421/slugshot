## Context

The separate first-person rig recreated shoulder placement, arm proportions and running motion. Repeated adjustments produced foreshortened paddles and a stiff raised idle rather than the approved third-person movement.

## Options

- Continue maintaining a second arm rig and map complete character transforms into it.
- Display the actual character arms and held gun, sharing all animation and attachment logic.

## Decision

Use the actual character limbs and held blaster in first person. The existing Presentation and BlasterAnimator modules remain the sole pose owners. Viewmodel now manages local visibility and the real muzzle position; it does not scale, reposition or animate arms. Roblox's head/torso transparency stays under the camera controller. Limbs fade only near the eye to prevent acrobatic clipping. Remove the separate viewmodel recoil/charge motion calls; the existing body aiming and draw/holster animation remain shared. The stable flip camera is retained.

This is an experimental presentation choice in the temporary range, with no gameplay or final art decision. The previous source is checkpointed under ignored build storage.

## Consequences and validation

Idle, running, sprinting and acrobatics use the same limbs, dimensions, shoulder positions and hand attachment in both camera modes. Hanging arms and a hip-held gun may sit below the forward view; raising to aim uses the existing BlasterPoint pose. FOV now affects the real body through normal perspective. Full verify_combat.py passes formatting, lint, strict types and builds. Studio playback, near-eye fading, aim transitions, world clipping, holster/respawn and owner appearance approval remain unresolved; no Studio control tool is available in this session.
