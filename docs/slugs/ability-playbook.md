# Ability playbook

How to get a slug's abilities right in fewer rounds. Read this before proposing. It comes from what the owner actually picked and rejected, and gets a new row or rule after every slug. Current picks and rules are in [ability planning](ability-planning.md).

## Rules

1. **A main is a plain hit plus one clear thing.** Every picked main fits: a wave carries on, a bind, a splash, an arc, a shove, a breath. Rejected mains added a second system: Prismfang, Ice Echo, Hailbreak, Spillover.
2. **A passive is a familiar tool for the map.** Sight block, cover, movement, a hazard, a wall, breaking terrain. Each could be a hero-shooter ability. Ask "what game has something like this?" The owner's own reference for Fire Curtain was a Valorant ability. If nothing like it exists, there may be "a reason no one's made an ability like that".
3. **Say it in one sentence.** If it needs a second sentence to explain how it works, simplify it.
4. **The element and the character must do the work.** It should be obvious why this slug has it. Old Inferno abilities failed for being "too basic" for him while suiting other slugs better. Gathering failed on Aquabeek for the same reason. Basic is fine when it suits the character: Rammstone just punches.
5. **Useful alone.** Combos are a bonus. Undertide failed for needing another slug.
6. **Check [who already does what](ability-planning.md#who-already-does-what)** before proposing. A repeat fails whatever its element. The owner may knowingly accept an overlap: Ice Wall and Rammstone's rock wall.
7. **Keep it realistic to build.** No slug that aims, flies or decides by itself unless the owner asks. Raise build difficulty up front, then respect the owner's call: tracking breath stayed because they wanted it.
8. **Concepts only, surface level.** No damage or balance numbers, and no in-depth rules for how abilities work together yet; the owner will do those later. Don't check whether a current map supports it; maps get built around abilities.
9. **Fewer, sharper options.** Two or three of each, ideally. Name one recommended pair and say why, with a few techs.
10. **One main can branch on what it hits.** Mending Shot does different things on a teammate and an enemy, so it always does something useful. Look for this before offering two separate mains.
11. **Passive and main can share a theme, but must do different things.** Healing Spring worked as the weaker, area version of Mending Shot, but Reek and Nauseate failed for being "too similar to the stink cloud". Check every main against the passive options in the same round. The owner preferred a shared space (a spring) to something personal (a pod).
12. **Ground every element rule in two named abilities.** Say which ability meets which, what happens in a match and why. The owner rejected a chart built from general element logic ("fire boosts gas", "rock reduces electricity") because it did not say how our abilities would do it. **Give each slug an element rule.** Healing clears burns and binds, water clears fire, electricity amplifies water. Say what the new slug clears, boosts or is weak to, and add it to [the interaction table](ability-planning.md#confirmed---abilities-interact-2026-11-05).
13. **Every shot should do something.** Rammstone's plain-wall rubble came from this. Ask what happens on a plain surface.
14. **Movement is welcome, not automatic.** Silkline and Updraft won; Bloom Pad and Lightstep lost to control picks (Bramble, Flash). Always offer one movement option, but don't rank it first by default.
15. **The owner likes disrupting the opponent.** Tangle, Flash, Bramble and Stink Cloud each mess with the enemy's shot, sight, movement or healing. A familiar disruption from hero shooters beat a cleverer idea every time in the round of 2026-11-05, and Sticky Bomb beat Fuse the same way. Recommend the most familiar strong option first.
16. **Invite the owner's own vision, and take it over any proposal.** Owner ideas won or reshaped three passives and one whole kit. A half-formed idea is still direction: build it next.

## Doing the work

- Record a pick the moment it's made, before moving on. Frostcrawler's choice was once lost when a session ended.
- A later decision wins, but keep the earlier one as history. Backdraft was "set in stone" and then replaced.
- Look at the lab's frames, not only its checks. Passing checks missed an arch that read as one block and an "electrical" caption copied onto a rock slug.
- When reusing a study as a template, search it for the old slug's words.
- When one option moves a shared object, such as a piece of cover, reset it in every other option of that study.
- Check each new option against the other proposals in the same round too, not only the confirmed roster: Nauseate and Tangle overlap.
- Floppy has no abilities because it never transforms. Check whether a slug transforms before planning its kit.
- After a second rejected set, stop building studies: offer a wide one-line menu and ask for the owner's picture, then build only what they choose.
- Reviewing several slugs at once worked: the owner picked quickly and rejected whole sets. Keep a rejected set's ideas as history and propose a fresh set.
- Read the side panel's text in the frames too. A one-sentence description once showed a doubled full stop.
- Be honest when the owner's choice differs from the recommendation, and don't relitigate it.
